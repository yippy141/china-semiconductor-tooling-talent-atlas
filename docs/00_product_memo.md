# Product Memo: China Semiconductor Tooling Talent Atlas

## Thesis
China's semiconductor challenge is often framed as a shortage of equipment, chips, and export-controlled inputs. The harder bottleneck may be organizational and human: whether China can train and deploy specialists in plasma physics, surface chemistry, thin films, vacuum systems, optical metrology, precision mechanics, process control, and production integration.

This project maps public evidence for that human-capital layer. It does not claim to measure capability directly. It asks where the evidence is strongest, where the gaps are visible, and which bottlenecks are most likely to persist.

## Audience
- Data journalism readers at places like The Economist or Bloomberg
- Strategy and policy audiences at firms like McKinsey or DGA-ASG
- Technical non-China watchers
- China watchers with limited semiconductor tooling expertise

## MVP Scope
- Geography: mainland PRC
- Industry: semiconductor manufacturing equipment
- Segments: etch/clean/strip, deposition, metrology/inspection
- Sidebar: lithography

## Out of Scope for v1
- GPU design
- EDA
- rare earths
- advanced packaging / HBM
- real-time claims
- individual scientist / engineer profiles
- exact capability scoring

## Product Pieces
1. Visual essay: 2,500-4,000 words
2. Interactive evidence explorer
3. Methodology and source ledger
4. Portfolio case-study page

## Evidence Principle
Use evidence strength, not capability score.

Example wording:
"Shanghai shows strong public evidence for metrology-relevant human-capital formation."

Avoid wording:
"Shanghai has a metrology talent score of 87."

## Kill / Pivot Criteria
- Fewer than 40 credible sources after focused search
- Fewer than 100 source-backed observations without padding
- Dashboard becomes a generic university/fab directory
- Visuals do not change the reader's understanding
- Thesis cannot be explained in two sentences
- More than two days spent debugging maps before data model is stable
