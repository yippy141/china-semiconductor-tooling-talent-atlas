# Copy bank

Use this file to replace vague or defensive language.

## New homepage copy

### Hero title

Can China Staff Its Chip-Tooling Push?

### Hero deck

Public filings and company records show Chinese toolmakers adding R&D scale and product breadth. The harder test is whether they are building field engineers, service teams, calibration routines, and customer-support capacity.

### Intro paragraph

China's semiconductor-equipment push is usually tracked through product announcements, export controls, and market share. Those are necessary indicators. They do not show whether a firm can install a tool, tune it at a customer site, keep chambers matched, recover after maintenance, and support repeat deployment.

### Method footnote

Public records only. Counts refer to records in this dataset, not workforce totals or technical performance.

## Better section headings

Use:

- The workforce test behind China's tool push
- Why broad STEM numbers are too blunt
- What each tool family needs
- What listed firms disclose
- Chinese toolmakers to watch
- Where public records go quiet
- Open the source trail

Avoid:

- An evidence ladder, not a leaderboard
- Geography is clustered but visibility is not depth
- Shortage lists are directional, not a census
- Look for scarce combinations
- What tooling talent actually means
- How different readers should use the atlas

## Replace these phrases

| Weak phrase | Use instead |
|---|---|
| public evidence signal | public record, source record, filing, product page, shortage list |
| visible layer | public record |
| conversion layer | customer-site support, service function, field application work |
| generic STEM supply | broad STEM counts |
| firm absorption | whether firms hire and train people who support tools in fabs |
| scarce combinations | cross-discipline teams |
| surface | show, disclose, publish, list |
| sharpen | clarify, test, compare |
| leverage | use |
| robust | strong, large, source-checked, recurring |
| nuanced | specific, mixed, uneven |
| landscape | sector, market, field, industry |
| important to note | cut the phrase |
| why this matters | cut the label and state the consequence |
| not a census | put in a footnote, not a title |

## Rewrite examples

Current:

> Look for scarce combinations, not generic STEM supply.

Better:

> The hard roles combine disciplines: plasma with RF hardware, ALD chemistry with vacuum behavior, optics with algorithms, and precision motion with calibration.

Current:

> Shortage lists are directional, not a census.

Better:

> Local shortage lists show what officials want to attract and subsidize. They do not measure the full labor market.

Current:

> Geography is clustered but visibility is not depth.

Better:

> Shanghai and Beijing produce the most records in this dataset. That reflects filings, parks, and local notices, not a measured count of engineers.

Current:

> The stronger signal is whether a firm is building the people and routines that move a tool from lab result to customer-site operation.

Better:

> The records to watch are field application hiring, service training, repeat-order language, and customer validation by tool family.

Current:

> Generic STEM counts miss them.

Better:

> A STEM total will not tell you whether a firm has plasma engineers, ALD chemists, optical metrology staff, and field service teams in the same organization.

## Caveat placement

Keep caveats in three places:

1. Footer footnote on homepage.
2. Methodology page.
3. Source or chart notes.

Do not put caveats in:

1. Hero title.
2. Main section headings.
3. Card titles.
4. Primary buttons.

## Tone test

Ask this before publishing a sentence:

Would a Bloomberg reporter, Eurasia Group analyst, or McKinsey manager say this in a client meeting?

If not, rewrite it with a noun and a verb.
