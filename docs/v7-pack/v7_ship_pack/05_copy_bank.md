## Argument section

Title:
The public record shows scale before customer-site depth.

Lede:
Most coverage of China's chip-tooling push tracks product launches: a new etch platform from Shanghai, a deposition tool out of Shenyang, a metrology product tied to a domestic fab. Those launches matter. They describe only the first half of what a foundry needs. The vendor-side question is whether the firm shipping the tool can install it, tune it, match it across chambers, recover it after maintenance, and support repeat deployment inside a customer's ramp.

Findings:

Broad STEM totals are the wrong denominator.
Etch, deposition, metrology, and lithography-adjacent tools each draw on a different mix of plasma, thin-film, optical, controls, and field-support talent. A national graduate total collapses those mixes into one number.

Listed-firm filings are the clearest workforce window.
AMEC, ACM Research Shanghai, NAURA, and Piotech disclose more useful detail than education statistics: R&D scale, technical staff, degree mix, after-sales categories, and product-family breadth.

Customer-site support is the hardest layer to see.
Product pages show ambition; filings show parts of the organization. Public sources rarely show installation practice, chamber matching, field calibration, service training, or recovery after maintenance.

## Replacements for AI-sounding copy

Bad: Look for scarce combinations, not generic STEM supply.
Better: The hard roles combine plasma with RF hardware, ALD chemistry with vacuum behavior, optics with algorithms, and precision motion with calibration.

Bad: Geography is clustered but visibility is not depth.
Better: Shanghai and Beijing produce the most records in this dataset. That reflects filings, parks, universities, and local notices, not a measured count of engineers.

Bad: Shortage lists are directional, not a census.
Better: Local shortage lists show what officials want to attract and subsidize. They do not measure the full labor market.

Bad: The conversion layer deserves more attention.
Better: The records to watch are field-application hiring, service training, repeat orders, and customer validation by tool family.

Bad: Evidence strength is not capability.
Better: A record shows what is public. It does not show yield, uptime, customer acceptance, or production quality.
