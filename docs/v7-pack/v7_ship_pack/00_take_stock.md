# 00 — Take stock

## What is now working

The product has a real question: can China staff its chip-tooling push? That is much stronger than a generic talent atlas. The firm dossier layer also gives users a next click, which solves the earlier database-viewer problem.

The best evidence remains the listed-company filing layer. AMEC, NAURA, ACM Research Shanghai, and Piotech disclose workforce categories, R&D scale, technical staff, after-sales/service categories, degree mix, and product families. These disclosures do not prove capability, but they show organizational structure better than broad STEM totals.

The technical framing is also right. Etch, deposition, metrology/inspection, and lithography-adjacent tools need different role mixes. Plasma process control is not the same workforce as ALD chemistry, optical metrology, defect classification, or lithography-stage calibration.

## What still fails

The home page still has too many equal-weight sections. It says the same three things several times: broad STEM counts are blunt, filings are useful, and customer-site support is hard to see. Repetition makes the page feel generated.

The hero still has too many jobs. It has to introduce the project, prove credibility, present a question, offer navigation, and caveat the method. A professional brief should give one claim, one standfirst, one primary action, and one credibility exhibit.

The UI still carries too much card grammar. Repeated rounded boxes and uppercase labels read like a Tailwind dashboard. A briefing product should use rules, gutters, large type, captions, and a few high-value exhibits.

The caveats are necessary, but they should not carry headline weight. The method belongs in small notes and the methodology route. The front page should lead with what the reader can learn.

## The V7 goal

V7 should make the first 90 seconds work.

A reader should understand:

1. This is about the workforce and support organizations behind Chinese chip tools.
2. The key public records are firm filings, product families, city clusters, and role-family mappings.
3. The hardest layer to observe is customer-site support: field application, service, calibration, chamber recovery, and repeat deployment.
4. The site lets them move from the brief into firm dossiers, segment briefs, and source rows.

## The line to optimize for

> Product pages show ambition. Workforce disclosures and support categories show whether the firm is building an organization around the tool.

Everything on the homepage should support that line.
