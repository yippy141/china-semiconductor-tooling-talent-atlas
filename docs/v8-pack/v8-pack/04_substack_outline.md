# Substack Outline

Working title:
China can announce chip tools. Can it staff the support organizations behind them?

Core claim:
Chinese listed equipment firms now disclose enough R&D scale, product breadth, and technical staffing to show real organizational buildout. The harder layer is customer-site support: field applications, installation, service training, calibration, chamber matching, and repeat deployment. Those functions are where product announcements become fab tools.

Structure:
1. Opening: product claims are visible, support organizations are harder to see.
2. Why broad STEM counts are too blunt.
3. What AMEC, ACM Research Shanghai, NAURA, and Piotech disclose.
4. How to read the numbers.
5. Why the map is source coverage, not talent density.
6. What would change the assessment over the next 12-18 months.
7. Link to the interactive site.

Suggested lede:
China's semiconductor-equipment push is usually tracked through products: etchers, deposition platforms, inspection tools, and lithography workarounds. The harder question is organizational. A toolmaker has to install tools, tune recipes, match chambers, train customers, recover after maintenance, and support repeat deployments. Public filings now show pieces of that organization, but unevenly.
