# 00 Take Stock and Product Decision

## What V5 fixed

V5 fixed the biggest early problem: the site no longer opens as a raw dataset. The landing question, "Can China staff its chip-tooling push?", is the right public promise. It turns the project from a generic China talent map into a monitor for a concrete industrial question.

V5 also added the right structure:

- Article-led homepage
- Monitor as supporting layer
- Firm pages
- Segment pages
- Explorer
- Sources
- Methodology

This is the right architecture. Keep it.

## What V5 still gets wrong

The site still lets the dataset talk too much. Readers see rows, evidence tiers, visibility language, and caveats before they get a clean answer to: "What should I know?"

The core problem is not copy alone. The product still has too many competing labels:

- atlas
- monitor
- evidence brief
- explorer
- source trail
- dossier

A visitor should not need to learn the product vocabulary before learning the subject.

## The product decision

Make the homepage a short executive brief.

Public title:

> Can China Staff Its Chip-Tooling Push?

Subtitle:

> Chinese toolmakers are adding products and R&D staff. The harder test is whether they can build the field engineers, service teams, calibration routines, and customer-support systems that make equipment work in fabs.

Internal project name:

> China Semiconductor Tooling Talent Atlas

Use the internal name in metadata, methodology, and repo docs. Use the public title on the homepage.

## Primary audience

Choose one primary audience for this release:

> Corporate strategy, government-relations, and policy advisory readers tracking Chinese semiconductor equipment firms.

Secondary audiences:

- Think tank and academic readers who care about source method
- Data journalism readers who care about the visual story
- Students who need a guided explanation of the tooling talent problem

This choice matters. Corporate and policy readers need watch signals, firm pages, and source-checked claims. They do not need to see internal row mechanics first.

## Main thesis

China's semiconductor-equipment challenge is not only a tooling problem. It is an organizational problem. Product pages can show ambition. Filings can show R&D scale. The hardest signals to find are field application, service capacity, calibration, chamber recovery, and customer-site learning.

## What to cut or demote

Cut from prominent headings:

- "evidence signal"
- "visibility is not depth"
- "not a census"
- "not a leaderboard"
- "look for scarce combinations"
- "directional, not a census"
- "surface"
- "actually"
- "why this matters"

These ideas can stay in the methodology. They should not headline the site.

## New homepage order

1. Hero with the question and one concrete claim
2. Analyst brief rail with four findings
3. Firm workforce chart and denominator note
4. Toolmaker footprint grid
5. Lab-to-fab chain
6. Capability and role matrix
7. City map
8. Western comparator frame
9. Source trail links

The city map should orient. It should not carry the thesis.

## What not to do now

Do not build a Three.js command center this week. The idea is good, but V6 must first make the argument clear. Motion can come later as a desktop-only enhancement.

Do not make the PhD and institution pipeline module the homepage. It is a good follow-on report, but it needs a more careful source base.
