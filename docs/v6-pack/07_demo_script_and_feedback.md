# 07 Demo Script and Feedback

## Thirty-second version

I built a public-source monitor for one question: can Chinese semiconductor-equipment firms staff the roles needed to make domestic tools work in fabs?

Most coverage tracks products, export controls, and market share. This project looks at the workforce layer behind that: R&D staff, technical personnel, service teams, field application roles, calibration routines, and the disciplines behind etch, deposition, metrology, and lithography-adjacent work.

The site has a short brief, firm dossiers, segment pages, source rows, and a methodology layer. It does not rank Chinese firms. It shows what the public record reveals, what it hides, and what to monitor next.

## Two-minute boss version

This started as a personal project, but it maps onto a real client use case: monitoring Chinese semiconductor-equipment competitors under export-control pressure.

The site asks whether China is building the staff and support functions needed to turn product announcements into usable fab tools. That means looking beyond broad STEM counts. The relevant signals are much more specific: R&D headcount, technical staff, after-sales service, field application hiring, customer validation, calibration, and repeat deployment by tool family.

The current release uses public sources only. It organizes the evidence by firm, segment, city, role family, and source type. The strongest pages are the firm dossiers and the workforce section, which show how AMEC, ACM Research Shanghai, NAURA, and Piotech appear in public records.

The product could become a reusable monitoring template for other China critical-technology issues: advanced packaging, data-center power and thermal systems, critical-minerals processing, or AI hardware supply chains.

## What to watch during feedback

Do not defend the site. Listen for these phrases:

- "I don't know what this is for."
- "This feels like a database."
- "I don't know what to click."
- "This is interesting but I don't know what I learned."
- "This sounds like AI."

Each phrase maps to a fix:

- purpose problem -> rewrite hero and first insight
- database problem -> move monitor lower and make article stronger
- next-click problem -> add firm and segment links
- insight problem -> make the Analyst Brief more concrete
- AI-language problem -> run language audit
